How the radio works

A tuned radio moves between two modes.

⚡
Sympathetic: the gas pedal

Alert, fast, ready to act: for threat, deadlines, anything new.

🌿
Parasympathetic: the brake pedal

Slow, safe, ready to repair: for rest, digestion, sleep, connection.

The point

You need both. Trouble starts when one gets stuck.

Mode 1 · the gas pedal

Constantly in sympathetic? Ask yourself.

😤Do you feel irritable over small things?
📱Do you reach for your phone before you open your eyes?
☕Do you run on coffee and skip meals?
😬Are your jaw clenched and your shoulders up, right now?
💻Are you answering messages at midnight, in yet another time zone?
🌀Are you wired and tired at the same time?

Yes to two or more? Your heart speeds up, your breathing gets shallow, your digestion slows and your planning brain dims.

🧠 Neuroscience

The amygdala triggers the sympathetic system: adrenaline and noradrenaline within seconds, cortisol within minutes. High levels weaken the prefrontal cortex, the planning brain.

LeDoux, The Emotional Brain (1996) · Sapolsky 2004 · Arnsten 2009, Nat Rev Neurosci 10:410-422
After a few years

It starts to feel like your personality.

Mode 2 · the brake pedal

Parasympathetic: when did you last feel…

🎨creative, without forcing it?
🤝truly present with someone, phone away?
🍽️hungry, and tasted every bite?
😴rested, after a full night of sleep?
🌅calm, just watching the sunset?
😌patient, when something went wrong?

That is the brake pedal working: your heart slows, your breathing deepens, your digestion switches on, and your planning brain is back.

🧠 Neuroscience

The vagus nerve releases acetylcholine at the heart and slows it. Heart-rate variability, a marker of vagal tone, is higher in people who regulate their emotions well and is linked to better control of the amygdala by the prefrontal cortex.

Thayer & Lane 2000, J Affect Disord 61:201-216 · Berthoud & Neuhuber 2000, Auton Neurosci 85:1-17
What it gives you

Patience, creativity, and clear decisions.

Both modes together

A tuned radio moves along the dial.

Stuck low: shutdownHeavy mornings, nothing worth starting. You cancel plans and feel nothing.
Stuck high: overdriveIrritable, wired and tired, unable to switch off.
🧠 Neuroscience

Both extremes are signs of a nervous system stuck on one station. Shutdown and freeze are described as parasympathetic states too (polyvagal theory, still debated).

Porges 2011, The Polyvagal Theory

Your state decides which version of you shows up.

🧠 Neuroscience

Stress shifts behaviour from goal-directed to habit-driven. About 43% of everyday actions are habits repeated in the same context, and we infer who we are from what we repeatedly do.

Schwabe & Wolf 2009, J Neurosci 29:7191-7198 · Wood, Quinn & Kashy 2002, J Pers Soc Psychol 83:1281-1297 · Bem 1972, Adv Exp Soc Psychol 6:1-62

The connection

The vagus nerve is the cable between your body and your brain.

The longest nerve of the autonomic system: from the brainstem through the neck, the heart and the lungs down to the gut. When it is active, the heart slows, breathing deepens and digestion switches on. That is the brake pedal.

And the traffic mostly runs one way: about 80% of its signals go from the body up to the brain.

🧠 Neuroscience

Roughly 80% of vagal fibres are afferent: they carry information from the organs to the brain. Only about 20% carry commands down.

Berthoud & Neuhuber 2000, Autonomic Neuroscience 85:1-17

So how do we tune the radio?

Door 1 · Touch · move, then press

Move first. Then press one point.

In the Five Senses Formula, movement is the loudest volume of touch. Two minutes of shaking, dancing or fast walking discharges the stress charge held in the body.

Then one acupressure point: Pericardium 6, three finger-widths above the wrist crease, between the two tendons. In the book it is the point for the overwhelmed.

Try it nowStand up, shake your arms and legs for 30 seconds. Then press PC-6 on your left wrist and breathe out slowly.
🧠 Neuroscience

Exercise raises dopamine and norepinephrine. PC-6 acupressure is best supported for nausea; the evidence for anxiety is early and mixed.

Meeusen & De Meirleir 1995, Sports Med 20:160-188 · Lee & Fan 2009, Cochrane CD003281
Door 2 · Smell · breathe

Breathe out longer than you breathe in.

Smell is the only sense wired straight to the amygdala and the hippocampus, and your breath is the one automatic function you can steer by hand. One long exhale tells the vagus nerve to slow the heart.

Try it now, togetherIn through the nose for 4, out slowly for 8, three times, following the circle.
4 · 8
🧠 Neuroscience

A long exhale increases vagal activity and slows the heart (respiratory sinus arrhythmia). Olfactory input reaches the amygdala and hippocampus without the usual thalamic relay.

Russo et al. 2017, Breathe 13:298-309 · Herz 2004, Chem Senses 29:217-224
Door 3 · Taste · the order you eat

Eat in this order: fibre, protein, then carbs.

In Morocco: start with the salads, then the tagine, and keep the bread and the pastries for last. Taste the first three bites slowly.

Try it at your next mealEat the vegetables first, then the protein, and leave the bread for the end.
1🥗
Vegetables firstSalad, cooked vegetables, anything green.
2🍲
Protein nextFish, eggs, meat, legumes, the tagine.
3🍞
Carbs lastBread, rice, pasta and sweets at the end.
🧠 Neuroscience

In small crossover trials, eating vegetables and protein before carbohydrate lowered the after-meal glucose and insulin rise compared with the reverse order. Smaller spikes mean smaller crashes, and the gut reports to the brain through the vagus nerve.

Shukla et al. 2015, Diabetes Care 38:e98-e99 · Shukla et al. 2017, BMJ Open Diabetes Res Care 5:e000440

The trials are small and were done in people with type 2 diabetes. The link to focus and mood is the practical application.

Door 4 · Sound · slow rhythm, then silence

Choose a slow sound, then leave a silence.

Sound reaches your alarm before it reaches your thoughts. Slow, steady music, around 60 beats a minute, slows breathing and heart rate. And the silence between tracks relaxes you too.

Try it nowClose your eyes for 20 seconds and listen to the room. Notice the loudest sound, then the quietest.
🧠 Neuroscience

Auditory input reaches the amygdala through a fast brainstem route, so tempo and volume set your alarm before you decide anything. Slow musical tempi lowered breathing and heart rate, and pauses of silence were relaxing.

Bernardi, Porta & Sleight 2006, Heart 92:445-452 · LeDoux, The Emotional Brain (1996)
Door 5 · Sight · light and horizon

Ten minutes of daylight, before the phone.

Special cells in your eyes read daylight and set your master clock. Morning light anchors your sleep, your energy and your mood for the next 24 hours. Then, when your eyes are tired, look at the farthest point you can see.

Tomorrow morningWalk to the water with your coffee, face the light, and keep the phone in your pocket.
🧠 Neuroscience

Retinal ganglion cells containing melanopsin signal the suprachiasmatic nucleus, the master clock. A week of natural light exposure shifted people’s melatonin timing earlier, aligning the body clock to the solar day.

Berson, Dunn & Takao 2002, Science 295:1070-1073 · Wright et al. 2013, Curr Biol 23:1554-1558
